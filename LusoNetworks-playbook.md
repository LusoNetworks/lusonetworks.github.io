# LusoNetworks — Business Playbook
### Services, pricing, and how to find your first clients in Lisbon

*A practical starting guide for turning your IoT / networking / embedded skills into a paying gig. Everything here is a starting point — adjust as you learn what your market actually pays.*

---

## 1. The one-line positioning

> **"A real engineer who sets up your smart home, Wi-Fi, cameras and sensors — fair fixed prices, plain language, English or Portuguese, no lock-in."**

You are **not** competing with cheap handymen on price alone, and **not** competing with €3,000 domotics installers on scale. You sit in the gap: **more competent than a handyman, cheaper and friendlier than an integrator.** That gap is real and mostly empty in Lisbon, especially for the English-speaking expat / host market.

Three things make you credible and worth paying:
1. **You understand the tech** (you study it) — so you fix root causes, not symptoms.
2. **No lock-in / hardware at cost** — you're the honest option. This is your single strongest differentiator; lead with it everywhere.
3. **English + Portuguese** — huge for the expat, Airbnb-host and remote-worker crowd who currently struggle to find someone they can actually talk to.

## 2. Where the market sits (Lisbon, 2026)

Rough reference points I found, so your prices are anchored in reality:

| Reference | Typical price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Handyman, negotiated rate | ~€35–40 effective / hour; jobs avg ~€110 | Fixando |
| Full smart-home install (with hardware) | €500–1,500 basic · €1,500–3,000 standard | Zaask |
| Individual devices (hardware only) | plug €35–70 · doorbell ~€200 · smart lock €200–500 · camera €50–200 | Zaask |
| CCTV / Wi-Fi installers | quote-based, generally opaque and pricier | Fixando / VS Security |

**What this tells you:** the established players either (a) bury the price and quote high, or (b) are generic handymen with no tech depth. Your edge is **transparent, published, labour-only fixed prices with hardware at cost.** That undercuts the integrators on total cost while out-classing the handymen on skill — exactly the survival strategy you asked for.

### Rules that protect you
- **€45 visit fee**, credited on any job over €150. Filters tyre-kickers, pays for your fuel/time, and feels fair because it disappears on real jobs.
- **Hardware at cost, no markup.** Show receipts. This builds absurd amounts of trust — and you make your money on labour, cleanly.
- **14 days of free tweaks** after a job. Cheap for you, kills buyer's remorse, generates reviews.
- **Care plans** (€35 / €99 / €249 per month) — your path to *recurring* income. Even 5 host clients on €99/mo is ~€500/month of baseline survival money. **Push these hard with Airbnb hosts.**

### A note on money-per-hour
A "Fix my Wi-Fi" at €130 that takes you 90 minutes is ~€85/hr — well above the handyman rate, justified by skill. A €650 rental package might be a full day plus a follow-up: still strong. **Track your actual hours for the first 10 jobs** and re-price anything that dips below ~€30/hr of *your* time. Don't undercharge out of impostor syndrome; your training is worth it.

## 4. Legal / practical setup (do this early)

- **Register as self-employed** (*trabalhador independente* / "recibos verdes") so you can invoice legally. Businesses and hotels will not pay you without a proper invoice, and it unlocks the whole B2B market. It's cheap and you get an exemption on social security contributions for the first year.
- **Get a simple liability mindset:** you're touching people's electrics-adjacent gear and networks. Avoid anything requiring a certified electrician (mains rewiring, consumer units) — stay on the low-voltage / plug-in / network / sensor side, which is exactly your skill set and legally clean.
- **A basic public-liability insurance** policy is cheap and makes hotels/shops comfortable. Get a quote; mention "insured on request" once you have it.
- **Keep a kit list & van-bag** ready: label maker, cable tester, spare PoE injectors, Ethernet crimp kit, a mesh demo unit, zip ties, adhesive mounts, a tablet for Home Assistant demos.

*(This is general guidance, not legal or tax advice — confirm the current rules with Finanças or an accountant before registering.)*

## 5. Finding your first clients — the concrete plan

Getting the first 5 clients is the hard part. After that, referrals compound. Here's the order I'd actually work, cheapest-effort first.

### Tier 1 — Free & immediate (do this week)
1. **Marketplaces — sign up as a pro:** [Fixando](https://www.fixando.pt), [Zaask](https://www.zaask.pt), and **[TaskRabbit Portugal](https://www.taskrabbit.pt)** (active in Lisbon). You pay per lead/credit on the first two, but they put you in front of people *actively searching right now*. Categories: smart home, câmaras de vigilância, redes/Wi-Fi, handyman/tech. Answer fast — speed wins these.
2. **Facebook expat groups** — this is your goldmine. Post a friendly intro (once, not spammy) and then *answer questions* in: "Expats in Lisbon", "Americans & Friends in Lisbon", "Digital Nomads Lisbon", "Lisbon Expats Housing/Rentals", "Foreigners in Portugal". When someone complains about bad Wi-Fi or a landlord/host problem, help genuinely, then offer. English-speaking, tech-literate helper = instant trust.
3. **Airbnb / short-term-rental host groups** on Facebook (e.g. "Alojamento Local Portugal", host communities). Hosts have money, recurring pain (locks, Wi-Fi, guest issues), and talk to each other. One happy host → three referrals.
4. **Nextdoor / neighbourhood + r/lisbon, r/PORTUGAL** — answer tech questions, soft-mention you do this.
5. **Your own network** — every classmate, professor, landlord, café you frequent. "I'm doing smart-home and Wi-Fi setups on the side, send anyone my way." First jobs almost always come from people who already know you.

### Tier 2 — Low-cost, higher-intent (weeks 2–4)
6. **Google Business Profile** — free, and makes you show up in "smart home installer near me" / "instalação Wi-Fi Lisboa" map searches. Add photos, your service area, WhatsApp. Ask every happy client for a Google review — reviews are the whole game for local trust.
7. **Walk into small businesses** — cafés, shops, guesthouses, co-working spaces, small hotels near you. A one-page flyer: "Cameras, guest Wi-Fi, sensors, remote access — fixed prices, local engineer." Leave your WhatsApp. Target places with visibly bad Wi-Fi or no cameras.
8. **Instagram / TikTok, lightly** — short before/after clips: a dead-zone fixed, a doorbell going up, a farm sensor dashboard. Tag Lisbon locations. Not for going viral — for being *findable and credible* when someone Googles you.
9. **QR-code business cards** — link straight to the site + WhatsApp. Hand out generously.

### Tier 3 — Partnerships (the multiplier, month 2+)
10. **Property managers & Airbnb co-hosts** — they run dozens of units. Become "their tech person." One relationship = steady work. Pitch the Host care plan.
11. **Relocation agents & expat-relocation services** — they onboard newcomers who need Wi-Fi/smart-home set up in a new flat. Offer them a referral kickback or a bundled "move-in tech setup."
12. **Estate agents & interior designers / renovation contractors** — they finish homes that then need the smart layer. Be the specialist they call.
13. **Electricians** — partner, don't compete. They do mains; you do the smart/network layer. Refer each other.

### The outreach message that works
Keep it short, specific, human. Example for a host group:

> *Hi all — I'm a telecoms-engineering student in Lisbon and I set up smart locks, guest Wi-Fi, noise/occupancy sensors and leak alerts for short-term rentals. Fixed prices, hardware at cost (no markup), and I speak English + Portuguese. Happy to answer any setup questions here for free — and if it's useful, my site + WhatsApp are in my profile. 📡*

**Rules of outreach:** help first, sell second. Answer fast. Always ask for a review after a job. Never badmouth a competitor. Photograph everything (with permission) for your portfolio.

## 6. First-90-days plan

| Weeks | Focus | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Register self-employed · publish site · set up Fixando/Zaask/TaskRabbit · Google Business Profile · post in 3 expat + 2 host groups | Be findable |
| 2–4 | Answer leads within the hour · do first 3–5 jobs *slightly cheap* in exchange for reviews + photos · collect testimonials | 5 reviews, a portfolio |
| 5–8 | Raise prices to full list · pitch care plans to every host client · walk 10 local businesses | First recurring income |
| 9–12 | Land 1–2 property-manager / relocation partnerships · refine which services actually sell · drop the ones that don't | Predictable pipeline |

**Survival maths:** your rent + food number ÷ your average job value = jobs/month you need. If that's, say, 8 jobs/month, that's two a week — very achievable once the reviews and referrals start. Care plans are what turn "scraping by" into "stable," so treat every install as a doorway to a monthly plan.

## 7. Things to get right from day one
- **Answer WhatsApp fast.** Local-services clients hire whoever replies first. This alone beats better competitors.
- **Always send a written fixed quote** before starting. No verbal-only jobs. Protects both sides.
- **Reviews > everything.** Ask every single happy client, on the spot, for a Google review. Make it a habit.
- **Photograph your work** (tidy cable runs, clean installs, dashboards). This is your marketing forever.
- **Under-promise, over-deliver** on the first 10 jobs. Those people are your referral engine.
- **Keep boundaries:** you're a student. Cap your weekly hours, batch visits by area to save fuel, and don't take mains-electrical work you're not certified for.
